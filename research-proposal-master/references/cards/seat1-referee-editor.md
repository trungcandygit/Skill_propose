# Tier 1 role card: Seat 1 (referee + editor: coverage, letter, tone, claims, institutional claims, proofreading, anti-slop, English)

Load after `core.md`. Used by the Seat 1 panel agent and by the main session whenever it parses comments, edits prose, or writes or audits a response letter. Each line: rule, short why, `→` source for the full text. Cite IDs in findings; end with `rules checked: n/total`.

## A. Intake and parsing

- **S1-01** Read the editor letter first and treat it as a fresh referee report; when editor and referee disagree the editor is authoritative, and the response to that referee says so. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#decision-letter-conventions`
- **S1-02** Read the full manuscript and every table/figure file before proposing or judging anything; never comment from partial reading or from recall; if the draft is too long to hold at once, review it section by section against the checklist. → `skills/referee-report/SKILL.md`
- **S1-03** Atomise: E1.. for the editor, R1.1, R1.2, R2.1.. for referees; split compound asks ("add X and clarify Y") and multi-location comments (R1.3a, 3b); split multi-point objections into rows. → `skills/academic-paper/agents/revision_coach_agent.md`
- **S1-04** Keep each item's verbatim text, a one-line paraphrase, tone, manuscript location and commitments; copy or preserve the reviewer wording and the original language of the report unless the user asks for anonymization or summarization; merge duplicates as "Raised by R1, R2". → `references/revision.md#phase-1---parse-atomize-preserve-never-drop`
- **S1-05** Unknown reviewer boundaries or vague comments ("needs more work") are flagged NEEDS_CLARIFICATION with a best guess and a question; never invent reviewer structure. → `skills/academic-paper/agents/revision_coach_agent.md`
- **S1-06** Do not rephrase a comment so its meaning changes and never paraphrase a sharp criticism into a gentler one. Why: editors compare the quote with the report. → `skills/nature-response/SKILL.md#red-lines`
- **S1-07** Personal attacks are flagged unprofessional; extract the actionable content and answer that. → `skills/paper-self-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-08** Formatting-only comments or tracked changes (font, spacing, margins, line breaks, indentation, column or page layout, float placement) are automatically skipped in self-revision and not presented; every other tracked change in the body is a revision item presented for approval like an annotation comment. → `skills/paper-self-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-09** Commitment extraction: every imperative or implicit imperative ("please add", "clarify whether", "consider") becomes one commitment with a type (add_analysis, add_clarification, add_citation, restructure) and the evidence it requires (new table, new figure, methods paragraph, prose edit); do not judge reasonableness at this step; keep severity, obligation, cost and consequence as independent fields and do not infer work order or rank from them. → `references/revision.md#phase-1---parse-atomize-preserve-never-drop`

## B. Classification and strategy

- **S1-10** Classify once: type (major / minor / editorial / positive), category (misunderstanding, clarity_problem, evidence_gap, scope_mismatch, incorrect_premise, high_risk), severity (minor / major / blocking / unclear); section-map each comment and check cross-reviewer patterns. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#triage-workflow`
- **S1-11** Action by category: misunderstanding → clarify plus cross-reference; clarity → revise text; evidence gap → robustness (minor) or new analysis (major) or restructure (blocking); scope → polite push-back; incorrect premise → cite manuscript evidence; high risk → rebut or concede head-on, never deflect, never a partial answer. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#step-4--action`
- **S1-12** Concede when the claim outruns the evidence, a control or limitation is missing, the gap is feasible to fill, or wording caused a reasonable misreading: narrow the claim, add evidence, thank explicitly. When a referee raises a valid point, the revision should substantively strengthen the paper. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#when-to-concede`
- **S1-13** Push back only for a false premise, out-of-scope ask, a request needing a different paper, or evidence already present: acknowledge the concern, explain the boundary precisely, point to evidence, optionally add a sentence in the conclusion flagging the question, and do not pretend the request is unreasonable. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#when-to-push-back`
- **S1-14** If a reviewer misread, first ask whether the manuscript caused it and fix the writing; revise for discoverability (move the buried fact forward, add a cross-reference) and never imply a scientific issue was fixed when only presentation changed. → `skills/nature-response/references/tone-and-stance.md`
- **S1-15** Conflicting reviewers: flag contradictions, preserve both positions and do not pick silently; target the supportive reviewer, explain the alternative interpretation to the dissenter, do not make the paper worse to satisfy a minority view; ask the user before editing. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#handling-reviewer-disagreement`
- **S1-16** Impossible or out-of-scope experiment: PARTIAL / OUT_OF_SCOPE plus a softened claim and a stated limitation; avoid time, money, budget, convenience or ability excuses as the primary reason for not doing a requested experiment; avoid making incompatible promises to different reviewers. → `skills/nature-response/references/difficult-cases.md`
- **S1-17** Blocking issue first: ethics, compliance, data integrity, missing approval, unsupported central claim or fatal method flaw gets no confident final wording until the author acts; do not draft around missing compliance; do not draft final wording until the author supplies the facts (needs_author_input). → `skills/nature-response/references/comment-taxonomy.md`
- **S1-18** Show the user the full comment table and wait for confirmation; every item gets an explicit author choice (will address / won't address with reason / not on point); never default a missing choice and never infer a missing reason, target or replacement; a normal "will address" does not authorize changing a claim's strength. → `skills/academic-paper/agents/revision_coach_agent.md`
- **S1-19** Padding with robustness nobody asked for is penalised; add what the comments require and the 2-3 checks a referee will actually raise. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md`
- **S1-20** Reviewer-requested citations are added only if genuinely relevant and verified; do not imply coercion or reviewer self-citation in the letter. → `skills/nature-response/references/difficult-cases.md`
- **S1-21** Observational design plus an editor constraint: the response strategy explains that both support claim softening rather than stronger causal language; proactively discuss ethical or data-privacy considerations when the topic raises them, even if reviewers don't explicitly request it. → `skills/nature-response/references/intake-and-routing.md`

## C. Manuscript edits (doing them, or auditing them)

- **S1-22** Per-comment loop: show the comment, the original passage with location, the proposed change and rationale, then ask yes / no / skip / edit; apply only after yes; re-read the target first and re-present if the text drifted; when the comment asks for missing content ("add a discussion of X"), show the surrounding context where the new text should be inserted. → `skills/paper-referee-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-23** Minimal change: sentence-level edits, keep structure, labels, citations, voice and the same writing style and tone as the original manuscript; preserve structure: do not reorganize sections or move paragraphs, or rewrite whole paragraphs, unless the referee specifically requests it; do not make additional "improvements" beyond the scope of each comment; do not delete content the paper genuinely needs, only collapse duplication. → `skills/paper-self-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-24** Mark every new or modified passage (in LaTeX wrap it in `\textcolor{red}{...}` with xcolor loaded; in Word use track changes or highlighted runs) and deliver a marked and a clean version. → `skills/paper-referee-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-25** After each edit re-check transitions, terminology and notation nearby; if a body number or claim changed, re-check the abstract and its word limit. → `references/revision.md#phase-4---revise-the-manuscript-first`
- **S1-26** A comment about a table or figure: open the table file and copy exact coefficients, SE or t, stars and N; a requested deletion is shown and confirmed before removal. → `skills/paper-referee-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-27** If a referee asks for an analysis that is not in the tables, flag it to the user instead of describing results; with empirical work outstanding, do not proceed to text revision until the user has completed it, and on re-entry check the new results exist first. → `skills/paper-referee-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-28** When revising after a critical report, fix the underlying muddle (section logic) rather than patching sentences cosmetically; revisions should genuinely address the referee's concern, not superficially dismiss or sidestep it. → `skills/master-academic-paper-skill/rules/01_master_editorial_philosophy.md`
- **S1-29** Work on the copy: locate the source manuscript (read only, never overwrite); round X starts from the output of round X-1; all edits go to the round copy (main-rX.tex, paper_R1.docx), never to the source file. → `skills/paper-referee-revise/SKILL.md`

## D. Response letter

- **S1-30** Write the letter only after the manuscript is revised and its numbers are final; never write the response before deciding the manuscript revisions or a reply whose decision is still open. → `references/response-letter.md#1-before-writing`
- **S1-31** Structure: cover note (thanks, 2-3 sentence summary of major changes, convention: comments in italics, responses plain, page/line refer to the revised file) → Editor → Referee 1 → Referee 2 → summary of changes → new material; point-by-point responses, numbered or clearly separated, each immediately after its concern. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#response-letter-format`
- **S1-32** Quote the complete comment text, never trimmed, then answer; IDs match the tracker; address every concern and do not skip any reviewer point. → `skills/paper-referee-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-33** Editor-efficiency rule: the first 1-2 sentences state the action (agree or not, what was revised / clarified / declined, where); then substance with evidence; then location. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#editor-efficiency-rule`
- **S1-34** Each reply ends in exactly one of four: clarified in the letter only, revised in the manuscript only, revised in both, or respectfully declined with justification. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#response-principle`
- **S1-35** Evidence anchor in every response row: paper location, table/figure, citation, planned work with status, or `unresolved`; provide evidence (concrete data, specific numbers, tables, figures, citations or reasoning); never "we added experiments" or "the results show" without naming the artifact. → `skills/review-response/SKILL.md#evidence-anchor-rule`
- **S1-36** Reply with what was done ("we have added", active voice), not what will be done ("we will add"); do not promise future work instead of doing it now; no promise to "consider"; no deferring a blocking comment to future work. → `skills/rebuttal-writing/SKILL.md`
- **S1-37** New results in the letter give actual numbers read from the table file (coefficient, SE or t, significance, N) and cite the new table by its manuscript number. → `skills/paper-referee-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-38** If a claim was softened, say so plainly and early ("our wording was too strong; the text now says ..."); burying the concession behind three sentences of context, or a softened claim never acknowledged as softened, fails; do not hide a concession in passive voice. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#per-comment-discipline`
- **S1-39** The referee must be able to cross-reference the letter against the paper without confusion: every page, line, section and table reference resolves in the revised manuscript with identical exhibit numbering; when quoting revised text give the complete verbatim excerpt (never abbreviate, never ellipses or "[...]"). → `skills/paper-referee-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-40** Keep a tracker (ID | verbatim | type | decision | change | where | evidence | status: done / partly / declined / AUTHOR_INPUT_NEEDED); anything not done is visible in the letter. → `references/response-letter.md#2-tracker-keep-this-table-it-feeds-the-letter`
- **S1-41** Readiness label: ready_to_submit only if no item is draft_with_placeholders, needs_author_input or blocked; if the user asks for final submission-ready text while required facts are missing, say so and keep placeholders visible. → `skills/nature-response/references/qa-checklist.md`
- **S1-42** Length: be concise but thorough, each response self-contained; editors compress, so no 50-page letters; a high-effort comment can take 300-500 words, a low-effort one 100-200; when in doubt treat a comment as high-effort. The letter is a primary deliverable: referees judge the revision largely by it, so it must read as a thorough, self-contained account of every change showing each comment was taken seriously. → `skills/paper-referee-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-43** Deliver marked and clean manuscripts, the letter, new tables/figures, a one-page cover letter, and an updated replication package if results changed. Revised papers that need technical work are accompanied by a point-by-point response explaining manuscript changes, and resubmissions must seriously address referee criticisms unless the editor says otherwise. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#pre-submission-checklist`
- **S1-44** Appeals and revision responses follow different logic: appeal-like cases and transfers are routed separately, never the default point-by-point path; do not reuse template example data as fact. → `skills/nature-response/references/intake-and-routing.md`
- **S1-45** Rebuttal audit of an existing draft is advisory: per comment addressed / partly / missing, with gap list and risk flags (combative tone, no evidence, misread point); it never marks anything verified or ready. → `skills/academic-paper/SKILL.md`
- **S1-46** Contradictory reviewers and skipped items still get a respectful, substantive explanation in the letter; never dismiss a referee's concern. → `skills/paper-referee-revise/SKILL.md`

## E. Tone

- **S1-47** Respectful, direct, specific, non-defensive, evidence-led; acknowledge strengths first, then the criticism; tone, accuracy and traceability meet the standard of material that may later be read by editors, reviewers or public readers. → `skills/nature-response/SKILL.md#default-stance`
- **S1-48** Forbidden: "The reviewer misunderstood", "The reviewer is wrong", "We believe this is sufficient", "We have revised accordingly", "Thank you for the comment" with nothing after, "As everyone knows", "Due to lack of funding", "as we already said", "as should be clear from Section 3"; avoid venting, accusations, ignored requests or distorted paraphrases. → `skills/nature-response/references/tone-and-stance.md`
- **S1-49** Avoid over-thanking, vague promises ("improved the discussion of"), evasive wording, hype in reply to criticism, excessive apology, sarcasm, and arguing with a reviewer's tone or competence; never use hostile or accusatory language. → `skills/aer-rebuttal/SKILL.md#tone`
- **S1-50** Thanks: open with sincere, varied thanks where a reviewer helped, but thanks alone is never a response: every response needs a direct answer, action, location or unresolved flag, and no major concern is answered only with thanks. → `skills/paper-referee-revise/SKILL.md`
- **S1-51** Disagree narrowly: acknowledge the concern as reasonable, state the disagreement in one sentence, give evidence (manuscript, literature, scope), add a small clarification if the text invited confusion; never personalise; "we agree" is followed by a visible manuscript change and pushed-back comments get substantive rationale, not stonewalling. → `skills/nature-response/references/tone-and-stance.md`
- **S1-52** Do not borrow claims or copy source phrasing from templates, examples or other papers; source advice (for example Springer Nature guidance) is writing guidance, not journal-specific binding policy, so do not overstate source authority. → `skills/nature-response/references/source-basis.md`

## F. Claims and paper structure

- **S1-53** Claim audit per major claim: keep / weaken / revise / remove, with evidence used, missing evidence, overclaim risk and suggested wording. → `skills/paper-self-review/SKILL.md#claim-audit`
- **S1-54** Every claim in the introduction maps to a table or subsection; the abstract, introduction and conclusion quote the same headline specification; the introduction is exceptionally polished. → `skills/master-academic-paper-skill/rules/01_master_editorial_philosophy.md`
- **S1-55** One central contribution; get to the point and do not bury the lead; the main insight is stated in plain words in the first two paragraphs and the reader understands what question will be answered; no "mystery novel" style with the punchline at the end; readers understand the result before seeing the math. → `skills/econ-write/SKILL.md`
- **S1-56** Abstract: problem, approach, main result with a number, contribution; no p-values, test statistics, confidence intervals, interaction terms or significance stars (user rule: state magnitudes and direction in words, at most one or two key numbers); within the journal limit; do not mention other literature in the abstract (one prior finding to set up a puzzle is acceptable if brief); the proposed approach appears in both the abstract and the introduction; re-check after revision. → `skills/econ-write/SKILL.md`
- **S1-57** Introduction: motivation → gap stated explicitly → approach → contribution list of elements not present in related work → a last paragraph describing the structure of the remainder, customised to this paper (not generic "Section 2 presents the model, Section 3 discusses data") and mentioning every major section. → `skills/proofreading/SKILL.md`
- **S1-58** Motivation frames a real tension: identify the focal X → Y relationship and the actual finding (do not change the finding); give two competing predictions ("on the one hand theory A predicts X should increase Y, on the other hand theory B predicts it should decrease Y"), each one a reasonable referee in the field would find plausible and each cited; no strawman alternatives and no invented mechanisms not supported by the economics, finance or accounting literature (search for citations if unsure). → `skills/paper-polish/SKILL.md#check-6-theoretical-tension-in-introduction-motivation`
- **S1-59** Section roles: the introduction briefly motivates, summarizes the contribution and previews findings but does not rehearse the literature in detail; the literature review organizes prior work and identifies the gap but does not re-motivate the paper; hypotheses development derives testable predictions from a tight theoretical argument and cites prior work without re-summarizing it; no cross-section repetition or copy-pasted earlier text. → `skills/paper-polish/SKILL.md#check-17-cross-section-repetition-introduction--literature-review--hypotheses`
- **S1-60** Literature: for each paper explain what they did and what limitation remains, do not just state their finding; be generous with credit, never insulting, never "everyone was wrong"; do not manufacture novelty by flattening prior work. The empirical papers reviewed earlier come back where the findings are read: set each key result against the closest prior estimates (agree, differ, and why), so the main empirical references are preferably cited two or three times (review, then results or discussion) rather than once in the review only; theory and method papers have no count requirement (user preference, BAI_VALUATION 2026-10-24). `scripts/check_docx_style.py` check 9 lists works never revisited after Section 3. → `skills/econ-write/SKILL.md`; `skills/master-academic-paper-skill/section_playbooks/06_results_and_mechanisms.md#3-reporting-null-and-inconclusive-findings`
- **S1-61** Results prose interprets, not transcribes: lead with the economic claim; give each headline coefficient its magnitude in native units, relative to the sample mean or SD, and relative to the literature or a policy lever (`skills/aer-paper-body/SKILL.md#magnitude-interpretation`); keep coefficient or t (not both) unless magnitude is the point, collapse parallel columns; preserve numerical accuracy (a kept number matches the table; "significant at X% level" is consistent with the underlying t-value); do not restate all findings verbatim (one statement in the abstract, one in the introduction, once more in the body). Measurable cap: about three numbers per results paragraph (headline magnitude plus its p-value or interval), the rest left to the cited exhibit; enforce it with `scripts/check_docx_style.py` check 8 (>6 decimals per paragraph), because reading alone missed it. → `skills/paper-polish/SKILL.md#check-16-concise-results-reporting`
- **S1-62** Mechanisms test specific channels (theory predicts M → if M, we observe X → test X); do not list untested mechanisms; generate testable predictions explicitly and, if untested, state what data would be needed. → `skills/econ-write/SKILL.md`
- **S1-63** Identification threats are addressed in the text: reverse causality, unobserved heterogeneity, measurement error; do not claim causality without clearly explaining the identification strategy; do not include all determinants of Y as controls; data limitations and winsorization (1%/99% is expected and must be reported) go in the text, not buried in footnotes. → `skills/econ-write/SKILL.md`
- **S1-64** Conclusion: summary, implications, concrete future research (describe what the extension would look like, not "I leave X for future research"), one page, findings phrased differently from the abstract and introduction; project confidence, avoid a generic limitations or caveats dump that undermines the findings, but a brief, specific limitations note is fine; do not speculate beyond what the data or model show. → `skills/econ-write/SKILL.md`
- **S1-65** Headings state claims, not topics; each paragraph opens with an assertion that the rest supports; paragraphs flow logically from one to the next. → `skills/master-academic-paper-skill/rules/01_master_editorial_philosophy.md`
- **S1-66** Main paper stands alone without the appendix; appendix and online-supplement tables and figures are numbered A1, A2 and each item is still referenced in the main text. → `skills/econ-write/SKILL.md`
- **S1-67** Do not dress up the paper to look impressive; no jargon beyond what a smart college-educated non-economist can follow; every sentence must say something. → `skills/econ-write/SKILL.md`

## G. Proofreading

- **S1-68** Define every variable and symbol the first time it appears (do not make readers hunt through footnotes), with its meaning and unit; every index is defined in prose (t is the time index); notation is globally coherent: the same symbol always refers to the same concept and the same concept always uses the same symbol (β vs b, X_i vs x_i); subscripts match the unit of observation. → `skills/academic-proofreader/SKILL.md#task-3--mathematical-notation--equations`
- **S1-69** Abbreviations: the abstract is standalone and introduces its own abbreviations; the main body introduces each abbreviation independently even if the abstract already did; after the introduction the full term is not written out again and the abbreviation is used exclusively; correct a/an before abbreviations ("an NN"); no use before definition. → `skills/proofreading/SKILL.md`
- **S1-70** Every figure and table is referenced in the text, mentioned before or at the point the reader encounters it, and discussed (not only cited); captions are self-contained, describe what is shown (never a single word or fragment such as "Results."), state what solid and dashed lines represent, and say when a figure reuses an object introduced in an earlier figure. → `skills/proofreading/SKILL.md`
- **S1-71** Grammar: subject-verb agreement, comma splices, run-ons, fragments, dangling modifiers, confused words (affect/effect, its/it's, than/then, principal/principle); split sentences over ~40 words; "I" → "we"; present tense in the body and for figure/table references; American or British spelling used consistently; hyphenate compound adjectives; avoid "'s" on inanimate nouns. → `skills/academic-proofreader/SKILL.md#task-1--language-grammar--style`
- **S1-72** Flag passive voice only when it hides the actor; method sections describe what the approach does and how it works, not re-argue why the problem is worth solving. → `skills/proofreading/SKILL.md`
- **S1-73** Consistency: one percent style, numbers under 10 spelled out, one date format, Oxford comma either always or never, paired brackets and quotes, consistent spacing around equations and dashes. → `skills/academic-proofreader/SKILL.md#task-6--formatting`
- **S1-74** Econ terminology precise: ITT, LATE, ATE/ATT, elasticity vs semi-elasticity, percent vs percentage point, units for every coefficient, correlation never conflated with causation. → `skills/academic-proofreader/SKILL.md#task-2--economics-terminology--precision`
- **S1-75** A quantitative result stated in prose is immediately supported by a number in the same sentence; "state-of-the-art", "prove", "cannot" need a citation, number or proof; if a figure or table shows no uncertainty, the text explicitly states why uncertainty is not shown. → `skills/proofreading/SKILL.md`
- **S1-76** Report each finding with exact location, snippet and concrete fix, ranked by severity ([ERROR] clear rule violation such as an abbreviation never introduced or an undefined symbol; [WARN]; [INFO]); when in doubt flag it; cross-propagate an error found once; judgement calls ("Note that", style choices) are flagged for author review, not auto-classified as errors. → `skills/academic-proofreader/SKILL.md#step-2--master-editor`
- **S1-77** Interactive fixes are applied one at a time after confirmation; do not batch-apply multiple edits without user confirmation between each one; never batch approvals. → `skills/proofreading/SKILL.md`
- **S1-78** Citation surface forms: one style (Author (Year) vs (Author, Year)); well-known results (FWL, famous natural experiments) are cited; working papers now published are updated; reference entries carry author, title, journal, year, volume; orphans (entries never cited, cites missing from the list) are flagged. APA 7 when the journal uses APA: "&" inside parentheses and "and" in running text; journal volume in italics; report and working-paper titles in italics; omit the publisher when it is the author; "Advance online publication." without pages until a volume exists (check Crossref); first authors who share a surname get initials in every citation (APA manual 8.20); a non-English title carries an English translation in square brackets (APA manual 9.38); add a URL only after it resolves or is indexed from the official domain (core C-05). → `skills/academic-paper/references/apa7_extended_guide.md`; `skills/deep-research/references/apa7_style_guide.md`; `skills/academic-proofreader/SKILL.md#task-5--citations--references`
- **S1-79** Equations: introduce each verbally before displaying it ("Firm i's profit is ..."), make it part of the sentence and punctuate it (a displayed equation is followed by a period or comma where the sentence requires one); do not display trivial equations that can be stated in words; inline symbols are in math mode; one bold convention for vectors; decorations (hat = estimate, bar = mean) keep one meaning. → `skills/proofreading/SKILL.md`
- **S1-109** Footnotes: one footnote per logical aside, do not merge unrelated asides; keep citations and cross-references intact when moving content into a footnote; no footnotes for parenthetical comments that belong in the text. → `skills/paper-polish/SKILL.md`

## H. Anti-slop (all new text, including the letter; never the quoted reviewer text)

- **S1-80** Avoid the em dash: zero em dashes in revised or newly written prose (—, LaTeX `---`, spaced ` -- `), including text inside red markup; replace by comma, parentheses, colon, semicolon or a new sentence; verify by search, not by eye. → `skills/paper-polish/SKILL.md#check-18-em-dash----usage`
- **S1-81** Cut throat-clearing and meta-commentary: "It is important to note", "It should be noted that", "It is worth noting", "In this section we", "Here's what", "Let me walk you through", "As we'll see", "The rest of this paper". → `skills/stop-slop/references/phrases.md`
- **S1-82** Cut vague declaratives and cheerleader endings ("The implications are significant", "plays a key role", "serves to highlight"); name the specific thing or delete; kill ornamental -ly adverbs by restructuring the sentence. → `skills/stop-slop/references/phrases.md#vague-declaratives`
- **S1-83** AI vocabulary to replace: delve, landscape, multifaceted, pivotal, crucial, groundbreaking, intricate, interplay, holistic, underscore, garner, foster, shed light on, pave the way, leverage (verb), robust (non-statistical), comprehensive, utilize. → `skills/econ-write/SKILL.md`
- **S1-84** Formulaic structures to break: "not X, it's Y", "not just X but also Y" (overuse), negative listing, rule-of-three synonym triplets, rhetorical questions, staccato fragments (don't stack short punchy sentences), Wh- openers, false agency ("the data tells us", "the decision emerges"); avoid perfect parallel structure in every list and vary the constructions. → `skills/stop-slop/references/structures.md`
- **S1-85** Vague intensifiers (very, quite, rather, really, simply, truly, deeply) are never used: emphasize through a shorter sentence or a quantitative qualifier and restructure rather than just deleting the word; no adjectives describing your own work ("striking results", "very significant") and no double adjectives. → `skills/humanizer_academic/SKILL.md`
- **S1-86** Rhythm: vary sentence length and openings (no three in a row alike), mean about 21 words, max about 40; end paragraphs differently. → `skills/humanizer_academic/SKILL.md`
- **S1-87** Never bare-delete a transition: when a connective goes, restore the logical link (In addition, In contrast, Thus) or restructure; keep one "Additionally" per paragraph at most. → `skills/humanizer_academic/SKILL.md`
- **S1-88** Keep terminology fixed: do not rotate synonyms for the same variable or treatment (no elegant variation); replace generic openers using phrasebank alternatives; "linked to" vs "associated with" chosen by meaning, not blanket swaps. → `skills/humanizer_academic/SKILL.md`
- **S1-89** Run the grep gate and report counts (em dashes, banned phrases, causal verbs) before claiming the prose passes; a mental pass is not an audit. Use `scripts/check_docx_style.py`. → `skills/paper-writing/SKILL.md`
- **S1-90** Preserve facts, meaning, data, numbers, citations, technical vocabulary and the author's voice; do not rewrite beyond what is unclear or broken; do not introduce constructions the author would not use. → `skills/humanizer_academic/SKILL.md`
- **S1-91** Optional score (Directness, Rhythm, Trust, Authenticity, Density, 1-10 each); below 35/50 revise. → `skills/stop-slop/SKILL.md#scoring`

## J. Next-round referee simulation (Seat 1 task)

- **S1-99** Simulate three hostile referees (identification specialist, field expert, generalist) with three priors and three reading orders; each writes independently, all three are drafted before reconciling anything, and no referee inherits another's findings; each must produce at least three major comments or explicitly certify, against their own checklist, why fewer exist; no praise sandwiches. → `skills/aer-referee-sim/SKILL.md#stage-2--three-referee-reports`
- **S1-100** Every simulated comment is specific and self-contained: it quotes the page or names concrete elements (table numbers, variable names, section and equation numbers) and states what evidence would resolve it; comments cover different aspects and never repeat one issue; every critique is grounded in what the paper actually says and stands on its own logic; findings of fact (a wrong conversion, a missing diagnostic, a scooped contribution) are verified before they enter a report, because a referee who hallucinates a flaw costs a revision round; do not invent issues. → `skills/aer-referee-sim/SKILL.md`
- **S1-101** Report the simulated verdict unsoftened; a simulation is a lower bound on readiness, never a prediction or certificate; never rerun until it passes without changing the draft; fresh reports each run; the simulation does not replace the consistency audit (typo-hunting referees are wasted referees). → `skills/aer-referee-sim/SKILL.md#honesty-constraints-for-the-simulation`
- **S1-102** Desk screen first: contribution statable in one sentence by page 3, cross-subfield interest, first three pages self-contained, design named on page 1-2, results converted to economic meaning, craft clean; if any desk-audit item fails the decision is reject: write the letter and stop; do not soften a desk reject into "borderline" to keep the simulation going; fix the draft and rerun. → `skills/aer-referee-sim/SKILL.md#stage-1--the-desk-screen`
- **S1-103** Calibration: referees veto, they do not average; any dimension at 0-1 or identification ≤ 2 of 5 means reject; typical failing signs are opaque sample construction with Ns that do not reconcile across tables, or a disorganized paper where the referee must reconstruct the argument; a minor R&R on a first run should be suspected as leniency: rerun with the referee priors sharpened; do not restate rubric numbers from memory. → `skills/aer-referee-sim/references/scoring-rubric.md`
- **S1-104** Scope audit: flag content nobody asked for (scope creep) and check mandatory declarations (C-17). → `references/panel-protocol.md`
- **S1-106** Institutional claims (core C-65 to C-68; Seat 1 owns the text side): every statement in the paper or letter about a regulation, mandate effective date, listing rule, comply-or-explain status, treated group or index/provider methodology is checked against a primary or authoritative source quoted with date; a claim the source contradicts, or a "voluntary" comparison group that also had a rule, is MAJOR (CRITICAL if the headline depends on it) and goes to Seat 2 for the coding check. Why: wording rules cannot catch a wrong fact. → `references/cards/core.md#g-institutional-facts-and-variable-coding`
- **S1-107** Exhibits as a reader: one key result in either a figure or a table, not both; a reader can write down the exact regression from the table alone; tables use booktabs rules (no \hline); figures have no in-plot title and lines thick enough to read when scaled down (full rules: tables-figures card). → `references/cards/tables-figures.md`
- **S1-108** Do not write multiple comments about the same issue; equations are part of the surrounding text flow; abbreviations: flag each full-term occurrence after the introduction point. → `skills/referee-report/SKILL.md`
- **S1-105** Writing a review report for self-revision: each comment identifies a specific issue, explains why it matters, and suggests how to address it. → `skills/paper-self-revise/SKILL.md`

## K. Resolved conflicts (stricter rule wins)

- **S1-110** "Thank the reviewer in every reply" (paper-referee-revise) vs "no thanks-only replies, avoid over-thanking" (nature-response, aer-rebuttal): brief varied thanks is allowed, but every reply must carry action, evidence and location; the stricter content rule wins.
- **S1-111** "No passive voice at all" (stop-slop) vs academic norms (academic-proofreader, humanizer_academic): flag passive only where it hides the actor or a concession; the academic-specific source wins for papers, stop-slop applies fully to the cover letter.
- **S1-112** "Delete all adverbs / Notably, Furthermore" (stop-slop, econ-write) vs whitelist of academic connectives (humanizer_academic): in finance papers drop them when no logical pivot occurs (econ-write, field-specific), keep them where they carry the logic, never bare-delete.
- **S1-113** Results reporting: t-statistics in parentheses and "significant at X%" without p-values or CIs (paper-polish house style) vs CIs and exact p-values (aer-robustness, nature-statistics): follow the journal; if silent, report SE or t as in the tables and give a 95% CI for headline and null results.
- **S1-115** Define before use (user rule): no equation number, coefficient symbol (a, π1, π3, c'), model variable or abbreviation appears before the place that defines it; hypothesis sections say "Section 3 maps each hypothesis to a coefficient" and the mapping sits right after the equation. Why: a reader meeting an undefined symbol stops reading. `scripts/check_docx_style.py` flags forward equation references and early symbols. → `skills/proofreading/SKILL.md#22-check-symbol-definitions`
