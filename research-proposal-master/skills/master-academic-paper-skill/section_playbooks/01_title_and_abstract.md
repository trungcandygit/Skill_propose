# Section Playbook 1: Title & Abstract

> The title and abstract determine whether referees accept an invitation to review and whether researchers ever cite the paper.
> Based on Bellemare's formula and Cochrane's direct-result principles.

---

## 1. Title Formulation Principles

### The Three High-Impact Title Archetypes
1. **The Finding / Assertion Title** (Preferred for empirical discovery):
   - Form: `[Intervention / Variable] [Active Verb] [Outcome]: Evidence from [Setting]`
   - Examples:
     - *Do Tariff Reductions Increase Firm Productivity? Evidence from Chilean Manufacturing*
     - *Algorithmic Trading Amplifies Volatility Spillovers During Market Crashes*
2. **The Mechanism / Tension Title**:
   - Form: `[Core Mechanism]: How [Channel] Drives [Macro/Micro Outcome]`
   - Examples:
     - *Liquidity Cascades: Information Transmission in High-Frequency Order Books*
3. **The Question Title** (Engaging, but must be answered immediately in the abstract):
   - Example: *Can Machine Learning Identify Structural Breaks in Asset Correlations?*

### Title Traps to Avoid
- ✗ Ban generic titles: *"An Analysis of Stock Market Correlations"* (Tells the reader nothing).
- ✗ Ban marketing buzzwords: *"A Revolutionary Deep Learning Framework for Trading"*.
- ✗ Ban acronym dumps: *"A Novel DCCA-MS-GARCH Approach for VN30 Asset Allocation"*.

---

## 2. Abstract Formulation (The 100–150 Word Formula)

### When to Write
**Always write the abstract LAST**, after the introduction, results, and conclusions are completely finalized. Extract the core sentences directly from the introduction's Hook, Method, and Value Added.

### The 4-Step Abstract Architecture (100–150 Words Max)

1. **Step 1: The Research Question / Core Problem (1–2 sentences)**
   - State the economic phenomenon, friction, or unresolved question directly.
   - Example: *"How do extreme volatility shocks propagate across equity sectors with heterogeneous liquidity constraints?"*
2. **Step 2: Empirical Setting & Identification / Model (1 sentence)**
   - Name the data sample, frequency, institutional setting, and identification strategy.
   - Example: *"Exploiting exogenous tick-level trading pauses in the Vietnamese stock exchange (2018–2024), we estimate a regime-switching detrended cross-correlation model."*
3. **Step 3: Concrete Empirical Finding with Magnitudes (2 sentences)**
   - State the central finding with exact numbers, signs, and statistical confidence. Say what you find, not what you look for.
   - Example: *"We find that cross-asset dynamic correlation increases by 38.2% during illiquidity regimes, driven by institutional rebalancing rather than fundamental cash flow news. The spillover effect decays within 45 minutes of trading resumption."*
4. **Step 4: Economic Implication (1 sentence)**
   - Conclude with the practical, policy, or theoretical takeaway.
   - Example: *"These results demonstrate that standard volatility hedging models systematically underestimate tail risk during market stress."*

### Absolute Abstract Prohibitions
- **NO citations**: Never cite external authors (`\cite{...}`) in an abstract.
- **NO vague promises**: Never write *"we discuss policy implications"* or *"results are presented and analyzed"*.
- **NO undefined acronyms**: Spell out terms or avoid acronyms altogether.
